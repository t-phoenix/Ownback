Ownback

Every purchase buys you a small piece of the company that made it. Shares land in your own wallet, on mainnet.

Hack window: Thu 5 Nov 21:00 → Sat 7 Nov 09:00 ISTStatus: v3 · user decisions + security audit, 6 NovWorking name was Hissa

Summary

You buy an iPhone. You snap the receipt, or Ownback finds the invoice in your Gmail. AI reads it, converts ₹79,900 to $955.00 at that moment's rate, and works out who is behind it: Apple made it, TSMC, Qualcomm and Corning supply it, and it contains gold and silver. 5% of the price (the default; each user picks 2–10%) is split 70 / 20 / 10 across those into per-asset USDC jars on Arc. When a jar is ready, you get a reminder (Sunday by default), review the live price and fees, and approve. Only then does it buy the tokenized shares and metals on the cheapest venue across chains (Solana via Jupiter first; Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum and BNB Chain as they come online), straight into your own wallet.

What changed in v3 (6 Nov). The user's decisions and the design-stage security audit (both in the repo: docs/03 and docs/10).
  • Every buy needs your OK. You sign a BuyApproval, and the vault checks it. Smaller jars can be bought on request.
  • Fees. Fees are always shown in $ and %, simulated before every money move, and capped by you (default 5% of the amount).
  • Ready minimum, not a per-company max. A jar is "ready" at $5 (user-set, $1–$50), which triggers the reminder. Any single company can take any share of a purchase. The monthly limit is the only hard spend ceiling.
  • Deposits. Arc deposits are used directly. A one-time auto-add on Base, Ethereum and Arbitrum moves later deposits into your vault with the app closed. Solana deposits take one tap.
  • Venues. Multi-chain, including BNB Chain.
  • Keeper. A fixed relay per venue with batched execution; shares still land only in your wallet.
  • Brand. Logo C, Ledger Green, Bricolage + Source Sans.
What changed in v2. The wireframes are now the source of truth, and this PRD was audited against all 27 screens. Changes: sign-in is Google (wallet tied to the account) or a seed phrase, not passkeys; deposits come from any supported chain; card/UPI is shown but off; Gmail invoice import moved into scope with its states; the supplier and raw-material split is core, not optional; every amount is shown in dollars, with local currency only on the receipt line; Home shows a total balance split into stocks and commodities, stablecoins and crypto, per chain; new screens for receipts, activity, settings, wallet, edge cases and the shared-link page. Problem and who it's for

People spend thousands a year on brands they love and own none of them. Investing feels like a separate chore: a broker, KYC, picking stocks. Saving apps round up spare change but don't connect it to anything you care about.

Primary user: a 22–35 year old in India or another non-US market who already holds a little USDC, shops online, and has never bought a US stock.

Job to be done: "When I buy something I like, let me own a bit of that company, without thinking about it."

Vault contract (Arc)

  • deposit, allocate(purchaseId, assets[], amounts[]) and depositAndAllocate, called by the user's wallet after Confirm.
  • execute(batchId, domain, orders), run by the keeper. Each order carries the user's signed BuyApproval (assets, amounts, venue, max price, max fee, deadline), which the vault verifies (EOA or ERC-1271). The money is burned by CCTP only to that venue's fixed relay, which can only change after a 48 h timelock.
  • receiveDeposit: credits USDC that a user's own auto-add forwarder (Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum) sent by CCTP.
  • withdraw / withdrawCrossChain: the user can always pull free USDC back, only to their own address (EVM: themselves; Solana: their registered address, 48 h to change). Money already in a jar stays until it buys or the user cancels that jar.
  • Spend limits: a monthly cap, enforced in the contract. There's no per-company cap (decision Q1). The invest rate is stored per user and must be 2–10% (default 5%).

Keeper

After a user signs a BuyApproval, the keeper re-simulates the route (price and fees), calls execute, waits for the CCTP attestation, mints USDC to our fixed relay on the venue chain, and swaps with the output going to the user's own address. It checks the token against two sources and the price against a reference. One batch per venue. Anything not bought (price moved, fees over the cap, swap failed) goes back to the jar at our cost. Changed in v3: a per-user Circle relay can't work, because Circle's Solana wallets can't run swaps and can't sign in the background.

Where to buy: permissionless venues

Buy each asset wherever it's cheapest all-in (price impact plus every fee), and leave it on that chain. A router picks the venue per asset from live quotes and the user's fee cap, and the user sees and signs the venue. USDC reaches every venue below through CCTP except BNB Chain, which needs an audited third-party bridge from Base or Arbitrum (Circle doesn't issue USDC there). Supply-chain basket ("Go deeper")

Owning the brand is the first layer. Behind every product are the companies that make its parts, so the user can choose to own those too. This also helps when the brand itself isn't tokenized: a supplier often is.

You buyBrand ownerBehind the scenes (examples)
iPhoneAppleTSMC (chips) · Qualcomm (modem) · Broadcom (wireless parts) · Corning (cover glass) · Sony (camera sensors) · Cirrus Logic (audio chips)
Dove soapUnileverSpecialty chemical makers (surfactants, e.g. BASF, Dow) · palm-oil processors · packaging (e.g. Amcor). Chemicals rather than pharma is the realistic link here.

How it works:

  1. Find links from real sources only. Use Apple's public Supplier List, suppliers' own annual reports (US 10-K filings name their big customers, e.g. "Apple was X% of revenue"), and Wikidata. A long-context 0G model (GLM-5.3, 1M tokens) reads the filing and must quote the sentence that proves the link. No quote, no supplier. This stops the AI from inventing supply chains.
  2. Keep only what's buyable. Each supplier goes through the same check as brands: is there a tokenized version with enough liquidity on Jupiter? Many suppliers (Foxconn, most Asian and European chemical makers) won't be. They're shown as "not available yet" and skipped.
  3. Add raw materials. The AI also lists the product's main materials (an iPhone has gold, silver, aluminium, copper and lithium; jewellery is gold or silver; furniture is wood; a car is steel and oil). Only materials with a liquid token get bought: today that's gold (GLDx, XAUt, PAXG) and silver (SLVon). The rest show as "not tokenized yet".
  4. Split simply. Default 70% maker, 20% companies behind the product, 10% raw materials, each spread evenly across what's available. The user can change it or turn it off. Each supplier gets its own jar, so the $5 rule still batches small amounts.
  5. Show the why. The confirm screen lists each supplier with its role ("makes the A19 chip") and a link to the source.

MVP cut: a nice-to-have. For the demo, precompute verified supplier lists for about 10 products (iPhone, MacBook, Dove, Coca-Cola, Nike shoes and others) and store them in the Arkiv registry as public supplier-of records with their source links. Live filing-reading comes after the hackathon.

Why judges should like it: it's a second use of AI that can't be removed. No simple lookup table knows that your soap connects to a chemical company. It also makes the Arkiv registry richer: a public, sourced map from products to companies that anyone can query.

How sharing works

  • Image: the server renders a 1200×630 PNG card at /card/[id].png using Next.js OG image generation (no design tool needed). Every share page carries Open Graph tags, so links unfurl with the card on X, LinkedIn and WhatsApp.
  • X: open the tweet composer with the text and link filled in (x.com/intent/post?text=…&url=…).
  • LinkedIn: open its share dialog with the link (linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite/?url=…). LinkedIn doesn't accept pre-filled text, so copy the text to the clipboard first and say "Text copied, paste it in".
  • Copy text / copy image: the browser clipboard (navigator.clipboard.write with a PNG). Where copying an image isn't supported, fall back to Save image.
  • Phones: navigator.share({files:[card.png]}) opens the native share sheet with the image attached, for Instagram stories, WhatsApp and others.
  • Default text: "I bought an iPhone and now own a piece of Apple. Every purchase buys me a bit of the company that made it. #Ownback". Each link carries a referral code, which drives the network effect.

Demo success metric: one real iPhone receipt becomes real AAPLx in a wallet in under 3 minutes, on stage.

"Own your life" works, but it's not the best main line. It sounds like a fitness or self-help slogan and doesn't say what the app does. What we believe

The people who buy from a company should be able to own it. Today the rewards of great brands go to people who already have brokers and capital. The customers who made those brands big get receipts. We think every purchase can be a tiny step toward ownership, held in your own wallet and visible on a public chain.

Mission

Turn everyday spending into ownership of the companies you buy from.

Vision

A world where customers are shareholders by default.
