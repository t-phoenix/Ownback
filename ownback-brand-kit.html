Brand core

What stays the same whatever logo or colour you pick.

Belief

Customers should own a piece of the companies they buy from.

Positioning

For people who shop online every week but have never owned a share, Ownback is the app that turns a small part of every purchase into real shares of the companies behind it, in a wallet only they control. Unlike cashback and round-up savings apps, the money goes into the things you already chose, and every buy comes with public proof.

Mission

Turn everyday spending into ownership of the companies you buy from.

Vision

A world where customers are shareholders by default.

Values

Yours, always

Shares go to the user's own wallet. We can't move them, and we say so.

Show the receipt

Every buy has a proof trail anyone can check: the AI run, the transactions, the source for each supplier.

Small, every time

2–10% of what you already spend. No big decisions, no market timing, no hype.

Messaging pillars

Own what you buy

Proof points: maker + companies behind it + raw materials; 2–10% you choose; works from Gmail or a photo.

Your wallet, your shares

Proof points: sign in with Google, no seed phrase; shares sent to your own address; withdraw free dollars any time.

Proof, not promises

Proof points: every buy has a public record; supplier links quote their source; no "returns" talk, ever.

Answers to the obvious questions

They askWe say
"Is this a real share?"It's a token that tracks the stock, issued by a regulated third party and backed one to one. It's in your wallet, and we show the issuer.
"Can you take my money?"No. Shares go to your own wallet. Dollars in the vault can only go to your jars or back to you.
"What if the brand isn't listed?"We tell you, and put that part into an index you picked, like the S&P 500.
"Do you read my email?"Only if you allow it, only order emails from shops, read by a private AI model, and never stored.

Voice and tone

We sound like

  • A friend who works in finance and never shows off
  • Plain words, company names before tickers
  • Numbers in dollars, exact to the cent
  • Calm about money; excited about the idea

We never sound like

  • A trading app: no "moon", no rockets, no charts on Home
  • A bank: no "dear customer", no fine-print tone
  • Crypto jargon in the UI: no "bridge", "CCTP", "mint"
  • Any promise: no "returns", "profit", "guaranteed"

Logo options

Three directions. Each works as a symbol, a lockup with the wordmark, and an app icon. Click one to load it into the preview.

Colour options

Each palette has five roles: ink (text), primary (brand, buttons), accent (highlights, the "piece"), paper (backgrounds) and soft (tints). Text pairings shown meet WCAG AA.

Type options

A display face for headlines and big numbers, a body face for everything else, and a mono face for receipts, rates and IDs.

Visual language

The motif: product becomes stake

An outline of the thing you bought fills with the brand colour from the bottom, like a jar. Used on Saved, Sunday buy and share cards.

Receipt details

Mono type for amounts, rates and IDs; a zig-zag edge on receipt cards; the ₹ → $ line shown exactly as the receipt had it.

Icons

1.75 px line icons on a 24 px grid, rounded joins. No emoji in the product. Company logos only as small round chips, never as hero art.

Shape

12 px radius for cards, full pills for primary actions and chips. One shadow, used only on the main action and the app icon.

Motion

One moment per flow: the jar filling after Confirm, the chip "landing" in the wallet after a buy. 200–400 ms, eased. Off when reduced motion is on.

Imagery

Real products and real receipts, photographed flat on a plain surface. No stock photos of people holding phones, no coins raining.

In use

The share card is what most people will see first, so it carries the brand. The preview at the top shows it in your current pick; these are the rules for it.

Share card

  • 1200×630, primary colour background
  • Headline: "I bought X. Now I own a piece of Y."
  • One chip per asset; the maker's chip in the accent colour
  • Amounts hidden by default; referral link bottom right

App store and social

  • Icon: symbol in white on primary, no wordmark
  • Profile picture: same as the icon
  • First screenshot: the share card; second: Confirm with maker, suppliers and materials
  • Banner: "Own what you buy." in display type on paper

Rules

Do

  • Write "Ownback" as one word, capital O only
  • Name the company before the token: "Apple (AAPLx)"
  • Show the issuer and the proof link near any holding
  • Keep at least the symbol's width of clear space around the logo
  • Minimum sizes: symbol 16 px, lockup 80 px wide

Don't

  • Use green/red for gains and losses on Home
  • Put the logo on a busy photo
  • Stretch, outline or add effects to the symbol
  • Use brand logos of the companies as if they endorse us
  • Say "invest", "returns" or "profit" in marketing

Name check before launch

A quick search found no app called Ownback, but that isn't a trademark search. Before launch: check trademarks in India (IP India), the US (USPTO) and the EU (EUIPO) in classes 9 and 36, and check domains (ownback.app, ownback.com) and social handles. The URLs in this kit are placeholders.

My pick

Chosen by the team (6 Nov): Logo C · Slice, Palette 2 · Ledger Green, Type II · Bricolage + Source Sans. These values are in frontend/design-tokens.md. The original recommendation is below for reference.

Logo A · Loop, Palette 1 · Cobalt & Coral, Type I · Familjen + Figtree. The loop says "back" without words, cobalt reads as trustworthy money while coral gives the "piece" a warm, ownable colour, and neither is the green every finance app uses. Familjen's slightly condensed, quirky shapes make big dollar amounts feel friendly. If you'd rather lean into "ledger and receipts", swap to Palette 2 and Type III.
